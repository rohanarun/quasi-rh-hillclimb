# A marked moment with separate bounds on the two ordinary lengths

Assume the preprint's $7/8$ theorem and the Eisenstein ordinary, inverse and detector inputs of tracker #143. Use the physical lengths $n,y$, reflected numerator $P_z$, denominator witness $S_z$, and prime sums $Q_j$ of [the joint physical reduction](asymmetric-ordinary.md). Write
$$
 A(M)=M/2-n,\qquad B(M)=(1/2+g_0)M-(1+2g_1)y,\qquad
 W=(A(1)+B(1))/2+21/10^6.
$$
The proposed root estimate is
$$
 \sum_{z\ {\rm kept}}\left|P_z(n,v) S_z(y,t)\prod_{j\in I}Q_j(z)\right|^2
 \ll_\epsilon U^{1+\epsilon}(1+|t|+|v|+T)^J.                       \tag{1}
$$
The kept set can be the physical sixth-power-free rows in the ball. The set $I$ is drawn from the original labels, with total width at most $W$ minus any sufficiently small fixed decrement. The denominator test may include its first log-norm multiple. The row and height constants may depend on the fixed target, arithmetic data and test profiles. The numerical margins and prime mesh precede the target. The proof below specializes the grouped ordinary argument to two coordinate bounds. Its Lean implementation for an ordinary face indexed by the sum of the lengths does not itself state (1).

## The reference rectangles

Put $u_0=3/5000$, $H_0=1-6u_0=.9964$ and $l=139/1000$. A reference pair has one length at most $l+o(1)$ and marked width at most $.101+o(1)$. At a grouped row ball of width $M\ge H_0-o(1)$ its nonexceptional energy is $O(U^{M+\epsilon})$, uniformly in both shortened lengths. Call its longer and shorter ordinary sums $L_z,S_z$ and the retained prime product $Q_z$. Cauchy gives
$$
 \sum_{\rm kept}|L_z S_z Q_z|^2
 \le \left(\sum_{\rm kept}|L_z|^4\right)^{1/2}
      \left(\sum_{\rm ball}|S_z^2 Q_z^2|^2\right)^{1/2}.
$$
The first factor uses the existing arbitrary-length unmarked fourth moment with its inducing-character exclusion. In the second, each normalized column has norm at most $U^{b+o(1)}$, $b=2(l+.101)=.480$, and coefficient bounded by $U^\epsilon/(NC)^{1/2}$. Fixed character factors stay on their columns. For columns $C,C'$ the product of the paired conductor and the redundant radical divides $CC'$ away from the fixed bad places. Majorize the ball by a common smooth positive radial weight. At a nonprincipal pair expand the extra unit mask by divisors before applying primitive Poisson summation. Its zero frequency vanishes. The ratio of the row scale to the product of mask divisor and conductor is $\gg U^{M-2b-o(1)}$, so smooth decay saves any prescribed power. On a principal pair the sixth-free parts of the two columns agree away from the fixed bad primes. Writing the good parts as $j^6c,k^6c$ bounds the normalized mass by $U^\epsilon\sum_c(Nc)^{-1}(\sum_j(Nj)^{-3})^2\ll U^{2\epsilon}$. The finitely many bad primes may be excluded in the columns or split off with their summable normalized powers. The same bound works at the root if an original coordinate is at most $l$. ## Retaining both coordinates through selection

Freeze the two upper lengths $n,y$ for one physical pair. The reference estimate and the [original-rectangle terminal estimate](asymmetric-child.md) allow bounded factors in either scale. Reserve a fraction of the terminal margin $7\cdot10^{-6}$ for the mesh and dyadic changes.

1. **Restore the cube-free source.** At each good prime the two ordinary variables are independent indeterminates $X,Y$. The unrestricted factor is $1/[(1-X)(1-Y)]$, and the cube-free unmarked factor is $1+X+Y+X^2+XY+Y^2$. The forward and reverse kernels are coefficient identities with the original square-zero mark labels. Each ordinary extraction divides its own scale by the norm of the extracted ideal. Each mark is retained or frozen. Replacing $X,Y$ by $\omega_1(p)X,\omega_2(p)Y$ multiplies every coefficient of bidegree $(i,j)$ by $\omega_1(p)^i\omega_2(p)^j$, of modulus at most one. This commutes with both kernel identities and preserves their absolute majorants, including zero multipliers. The two initially restored scales are individually bounded by their incoming scales. [Local identities][kernels] · [Full restoration][restore] · [Restored ordinary coordinates][rootstate]

2. **Install the reference at the restored root.** Where centering is required and both lengths exceed $l$, subtract the equal-product rectangle with first log length $l$ and second log length the original sum minus $l$. Keep the difference signed through both transforms. The separated reference is covered above, including after the forward kernel, which shortens the coordinates and drops marks. For $P=n+y<.802,\ S\le W<.101$, the ordinary root reserves at $\alpha=3/5,\ P_{\rm ref}=2/3$ satisfy
   $$
   \min\{1-P-S-4u_0,\ 5/6+\alpha l-P-S-3u_0,\
                          5/6-P_{\rm ref}-S-3u_0\}>.01 .
   $$
   Also $2l<P_{\rm ref}$. These are the volume, exceptional and second-diagonal conditions. A low root uses the uncentered remainder. [Reference conditions][gates] · [Actual recentered root][recenter]

3. **Select on each first-common side.** Let $A_0$ be the original total norm exponent, $c,d$ the two first-common exponents, $q_0$ their radical exponent, $f$ the frozen mark width and $K_0-K$ the first frequency defect. Select whole surviving factors up to $\min(u_0,S-f)$, with at most one mesh of overshoot, and write $u'$ for the selected width. If supply is below $u_0$, select it all and use Cauchy with the two arbitrary-length unmarked fourth moments. The native tiny-supply floor can instead be deleted at its trivial width cost. Otherwise $u_0\le u'\le u_0+{\rm mesh}$ and the live width is at most $W-u_0$. On the $c$-side the normalizations are
   $$
      a=A_0-c-u',\quad k=K+4u',\quad q_c=q_0+c-d,\quad
      K_0=2A_0-c-d+q_0-1 .
   $$
   Thus $2a-k+q_c=1+(K_0-K)-6u'$. The native frequency enclosure bounds this below by $H_0$ up to the mesh and frequency tolerances. The common ball is raised to this envelope. An individual label can have a smaller free row length. [Selection and frequency enclosure][freq] · [Two-side normalization][sides]

4. **Group before estimating.** The pairs supplied to the second step have the form $(X_1/N_1,X_2/N_2)$ and $(Y_1/N_1,Y_2/N_2)$ with $N_i\ge1$. A nonempty subunit scale can be clipped with a bounded change in its test. The label reduction returns a subproduct and two more scales, dominated coordinate by coordinate by *one of those pairs*. Classify by which pair, both dyadic lengths, the remaining slot indices and the fixed ray. The extra binary choice at most doubles the existing $O((1+\log U)^2)$ count for fixed labels. Within one class the true row is $z=V_0 A_2 h'$, with the moving twist $V_0 A_2$ included. The Cauchy weights for labels hitting $z$ have sum $O_\epsilon(U^\epsilon)$ after the existing extraction normalization. Their common factors and codes divide $(z)^2$. The other divisor has a harmonic weight $1/ND$. This includes a prime occurring once in both second common factors on the nonunit branch. An independent count of such common factors followed by a moment at the shorter $h'$ scale would lose a power. After grouping, the common-profile inequality takes the ordinary energy at one ball $K_c=2U^{M+O({\rm mesh})}$ with fixed character, moving twist one and natural zeros. The test lengths are within a bounded factor of the two class ceilings. Every original-shaped class retains both physical ceilings. Every reference-shaped class retains a coordinate at most $l+o(1)$. 5. **Insert the two positive estimates.** In that common-profile inequality use $U^{M+u_0-7\cdot10^{-6}+\epsilon}$ on an original-shaped class and $U^{M+\epsilon}$ on a reference. The former may dip below $H_0$ in its row width by a mesh error, covered by the $5/6$ slope estimate. The original price fits below $u'$ in the nonexhausted case. Exhausted and reference cases have zero excess, up to the permitted arbitrarily small losses. If the common ball costs $M+p$, the outer factor of exponent $k-a$ gives $a+q_c+p$ up to the same tolerances. After paying $p\le u'$ the two sides cost $A_0-c+q_c$ and $A_0-d+q_d$, where $q_d=q_0+d-c$. Their sum is $2(A_0+q_0)-c-d$, exactly the paired first-common normalization. The diagonal, zero frequency and centered exceptional terms use the root reserves. The coordinate-dependent estimate enters on an individual class, before the total-width maximum is taken. The earlier induction at a face indexed by total width would require control at additional balanced shapes. [Grouped side collection][assemble] · [First-side restoration][first]
