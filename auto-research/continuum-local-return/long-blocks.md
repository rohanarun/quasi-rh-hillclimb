**Lemma.** Consider retained rows having a saturated inverse block with length r in [1.96,2.16]. Saturation means that its centrally normalized squared magnitude is at least a fixed constant times U^(δr−ε). The block has one of the detector’s presentations with its literal zeros, a common untwisted smooth annular profile after the usual dyadic subdivision, and a rowwise real exponent in the detector’s compact range. Its norm twist has the form γ−ν with |γ|≤3iT₁ and |ν|≤T₁/16. Assume both buffered-bin guards of the source (the selected zero near the left edge and the zero-free rectangle to its right), and retain a nonprincipal original presentation. The index i ranges over the source’s fixed finite set. Before paying its height cost each moment carries its prescribed factor bounded by a fixed power of 1+(3i+O(1))T₁. Take this below the allocated U-power after fixing the preliminary loss, bin widths and smooth orders. Physical prime-slot heights are held in their row-independent coefficients when permitted by the moment, or retain the same explicit height payment. These hypotheses are needed for pointwise control of descendants. The number of these rows is O(U^(R−10⁻⁷)). The bound is uniform over the external parameters and the dyadic subdivisions in the same sense as the source’s row count. It also applies with O((log U)^C) allowed common dyads and profiles, with a fixed C and uniform seminorms, by summing the corresponding estimates. ## Smooth factorization and the three spikes

Fix A=U^1.10 and a smooth cutoff V equal to one below one and zero above two. For ideals put h(n)=μ(n)ψ(n), f(n)=ψ(n), and h_A(n)=h(n)V(q_n/A). Complete multiplicativity with the original zero extensions gives h*f as the unit for Dirichlet convolution. Consequently

    h−2h_A+h_A*f*h_A = (h−h_A)*f*(h−h_A).

The right side vanishes at q_n≤A². If q_n≍U^r, 1.96≤r≤2.16, the fixed annular support is contained in (2A,A²) for large U. On every such original block, the coefficient is therefore exactly −h_A*f*h_A. This argument uses the smoothed truncation without modifying any original Euler mask.

Insert smooth annular dyadic partitions on all three variables, including a unit dyad. For a nonzero block write X₁=U^x₁, X₂=U^x₂ for the two inverse centers and Y=U^y for the ordinary center. One has x₁,x₂,y≥0, x_j≤1.10+O(1/log U) and x₁+x₂+y=r+O(1/log U). The implicit constants depend only on fixed supports. The product of the three central normalization factors differs from the original by a bounded factor bounded away from zero. There are O((log U)^3) possible triples. The factors V((X_j/A)z_j) remain inside the two inverse profiles. Their logarithmic derivatives are uniformly bounded on the fixed supporting annulus.

The only nonseparable weight is the source’s common smooth profile evaluated at (X₁X₂Y/U^r) z₁z₂z₃. Mellin inversion on the logarithmic product separates it with **one common Fourier frequency** and a uniform rapidly decaying L¹ weight. The initial rowwise real and imaginary norm powers separate directly over the three factors. Truncate the extra frequency to T₁/16. The omitted integral is smaller than any prescribed negative U-power, since T₁ is a fixed positive power of U up to the bounded conductor exponent, the lengths are bounded, and the trivial absolute sums and the number of triples have fixed polynomial growth. Choose this tail order after the height exponent. The combined translations of γ, the factorization frequency, and the later pointwise Mellin variables fit inside the source’s single T₁/2 allowance. The detector can choose its first Fourier cutoff as T₁/16 for the same reason.

The original lower bound, the triangle inequality, the uniform L¹ bound and the polylogarithmic number of pieces give a single tuple and a common frequency on each row with

    |M(x₁) M(x₂) S(y)|² ≫ U^[δ(x₁+x₂+y)−e₁].

Here e₁ can be made arbitrarily small. The paper’s pointwise upper bounds for the two inverse factors are U^(δx_j+e₂), and the ordinary factor costs at most U^[δ min(y,1−y)+e₂]. Comparing the product with these upper bounds first gives 2δ(y−1/2)_+≤e₁+3e₂+o(1). Dividing by the bounds for the other two factors gives the three separate lower bounds U^(δx_j−e₃), U^(δy−e₃) with arbitrarily small e₃ after the earlier losses have been reduced. These new polynomials retain the same presentation and phase. For the following counts, freeze the finitely many presentation labels and all dyadic lengths and apply the source’s rowwise norm-twist Sobolev bounds. The Fourier frequencies may vary by row. All prime-slot coefficients remain fixed within each original bin.
