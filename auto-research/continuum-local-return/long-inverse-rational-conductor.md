# Squarefree rational norms in the long-inverse Fourier interval

Assume the preprint's $7/8$ theorem and the inputs of the [long-inverse reduction](long-inverse-frequency-band.md). Retain its $U,A,H,L,s,e,T$ and its genuine inverse test. A sufficient version of its outstanding signed estimate uses ideal columns with **squarefree rational norms**, with $\gcd_{\mathbb Z}(Nn,Nn')<B/L$ at column scale $B$. The product of the split rational primes occurring once across the pair is then $\gg L^2$. Each of these primes contributes local order six upon restriction of the paired character to rational integers. The frequency interval and the proved frequency deletions apply simultaneously. This version calls for scales $B=A/k^2>L$, $k$ a positive rational integer, in place of the single full-length test. No bound on the whole remaining frequency interval is known.

## Projecting the two columns

Off the fixed bad rational primes, an ideal-squarefree $n$ has a unique largest rational integer $k$ with $(k)\mid n$. At a split prime $p$ the integer $k$ contains $p$ exactly when both places occur. At an inert prime it contains $p$ exactly when the inert ideal occurs. Thus $k$ is squarefree, $n=(k)n_0$, $Nn_0$ is rational-squarefree on split primes, and $(n_0,(k))=1$. The ramified prime belongs to the fixed excluded set. If the bad set omits just one place of a split rational prime, the allowed place can occur only once in $n_0$. Keep that orientation and the original exclusion throughout; this preserves the exact scale $B=A/k^2$. For each $k$ the original column becomes a rational-squarefree inverse of scale $B=A/k^2$, times $\mu((k))\psi_v((k))/k$. Its additional puncture is precisely at $(k)$. The sum of the scalar upper bounds on nonempty scales costs $O(\log(2A))$ in the row norm. This reduction uses triangle before squaring on the positive physical row majorant.

The native raw estimate (1) in the [long-inverse proof](long-inverse-frequency-band.md) also holds on squarefree rational norms, including a polynomial-norm puncture. Within the genuine ideal-squarefree column, insert $\sum_{b\,:\,(b)\mid n}\mu_{\mathbb Z}(b)$ to remove all rational content. Extracting $(b)$ costs $1/b$ and leaves an ordinary genuine punctured inverse at scale divided by $b^2$; its remaining zero mask is handled by (1). The sum over $b$ is harmonic. In particular all $B\le L$ already meet the required physical price $HU^{e+\epsilon}$. At larger $B$ set $H_B=B/U^e$, $T_B=B/L$, $r_B=\log_U B$, $s_B=\log_U(B/H)$, so $e<s_B\le s$. Use the same subtraction with $A,H_1,T,r,s$ replaced in its Fourier calculation by $B,H_B,T_B,r_B,s_B$. Estimate (2) of the parent note is applied only once, to the reconstructed original column at $A$. It requires no new detector or lifting argument at these shortened scales.

To delete pairs with $\gcd_{\mathbb Z}(Nn,Nn')\ge T_B$, expand this indicator as $\sum_{b\mid Nn,Nn'} (\mu_{\mathbb Z}*1_{[T_B,\infty)})(b)$. Only squarefree $b$ supported on good split primes occur. The projection $b\mid Nn$ splits into at most $2^{\omega(b)}$ ideals $l\mid n$ of norm $b$. Each gives a scalar of modulus at most $b^{-1/2}$ and a genuine rational-squarefree column at $B/b$ omitting both places over the rational support of $b$. The native bound just proved, triangle over the orientations and summation over $b$ give $O_\epsilon(HU^{e+\epsilon})$ for this deletion in both physical kernels, with the reference kernel multiplied by $H/H_B$. This uses $B/b\le L$ when the inversion coefficient is nonzero. Every surviving pair has a product of solitary split primes exactly $Nn Nn'/\gcd(Nn,Nn')^2\gg L^2$. At such a prime the only local factor is a primitive sextic character or its conjugate at one split place. Rational integers surject onto its residue field.

## The frequency deletions on the same pairs

For rational-squarefree columns let $g$ be their common ideal and let $o$ consist of the prime ideals on the first side whose conjugates occur on the second. With $G=Ng$, $K=No$ write uniquely
$$
 n=g o a,\qquad n'=g\bar o b,\qquad (Na,Nb)_{\mathbb Z}=1,
 \qquad GK=\gcd(Nn,Nn')<T_B.
$$
The rational prime supports of $g,o,a,b$ are disjoint apart from the deliberately shared $g,o$ in the two original columns. For fixed $g,o$ the free norm scale is $B/(GK)$. The primitive ideal modulus is $o\bar o a b$, and its norm is $d=K^2NaNb\asymp (B/G)^2$ in the masked Poisson formula. The exact mask is at $g$. Thus the smooth multiplier and the interval $cJd/H_B\le Nh\le C Jd/H$ are as in the parent note for $j\mid g$, $J=Nj$. Its upper bound in each dyad is $O(J(B/G)^2/H)$. The six-unit condition also persists.

To see that the frequency estimates survive this restriction, use $\sum_{m\mid Na,Nb}\mu_{\mathbb Z}(m)$ *after* extending the expression on coprime pairs using its separated Gauss factors. In a summand, $m\mid Na$ and $m\mid Nb$ choose one orientation on each side above each prime of $m$. Denote the two fixed ideals by $m_1,m_2$, both of norm $m$. There are at most $4^{\omega(m)}$ choices. The free ideals on each side omit both places over $m$. For the first side put $o_1=o$, and for the second put $o_2=\bar o$. The opposite-column cross phase of the Gauss factor is fixed on the finite ray subdivisions by sextic reciprocity; the within-column extraction of $o_i m_i$ on its coprime support adds $J_c(o_i m_i)^2$ to the Gauss coefficient on the remaining squarefree ideal $c$. The frequency is $\overline{J_c(h)}$, and the mask term is $J_c(j)$. These are precisely the factors of the [unprojected frequency estimate](long-inverse-frequency-sectors.md) with $m_i o_i$ replacing its fixed ideal $m$. Fixed factors depending on $h$ have modulus at most one. The reverse orientation conjugates the complete side.

The rational-squarefree constraint on $c$ can now be imposed by $\sum_{b_0:(b_0)\mid c}\mu_{\mathbb Z}(b_0)$. Insert the remaining fixed ideal omissions with $\sum_{l_0\mid(c,P)}\mu(l_0)$, where $P$ includes the original and extracted exclusions and has polynomial norm. Together these impose $l\mid c$ for $l=\operatorname{lcm}((b_0),l_0)$. Ignore empty nonsquarefree cases. In the canonical column the scalar from this extraction has modulus at most $(Nl)^{-1/2}\le1/b_0$, and $b_0$ has polynomial range. The total scalar mass, also summing $l_0\mid P$, is $U^{o(1)}$. The existing symbols give zeros at $j o_i m_i$, so retained $l$ is coprime to this ideal. The canonical fourth-power label becomes the squarefree $j o_i m_i l$, the row remains $jh$, and the residual column length is $B/(GK m Nl)$. The literal zero on overlaps upon extracting $l$ is the factor $J_c(l)^4$ on the new free variable. The puncture and Gauss identities here are the ones in the [sector estimate](long-inverse-frequency-sectors.md#exact-child-polynomial).

In the notation of that estimate use $r_B,s_B$ and $d_0=\log_U(KmNl)$ in place of the logarithm of its extracted product. Its relation $x=r_B-\log_U G-d_0$, its label cost $V=\log_U J+d_0$, its upper bound for $Nh$, and the target one-sided energy $J(B/G)$ hold verbatim. These identities are also the only places the lengths of the extracted factors enter the grouped sieves. The common exterior factor before the $l$-weights is
$$
 \frac{H}{J(B/G)\,G K m}.
$$
Indeed the Poisson prefactor has scale $H/[J(B/G)]$ and the two original column normalizations after extraction together supply $1/(GKm)$. The energy estimate therefore costs $H/(GKm)$ per fixed allocation, up to the stipulated small losses. The three sums are harmonic, including the divisor weights, the $j\mid g$ choices and the orientations above $m$. For the large sixth-power sector write $h=v^6 h_0$ and fix $v$ before the projections on each one-sided Hilbert norm. The remaining column symbol is $J_c(jh_0)1_{(c,v)=1}$. Use $l_0\mid\operatorname{rad}(Pv)$ in the same simultaneous lcm expansion. Its mass is still
$$
 \sum_{l_0\mid\operatorname{rad}(Pv)}
 \sum_{b_0\ll B^{1/2}}N\operatorname{lcm}((b_0),l_0)^{-1/2}
 \ll \tau(\operatorname{rad}(Pv))\log(2B)=U^{o(1)}
$$
uniformly at fixed $v$. Thus $k=jh_0$ and $f=jo_i m_i l$ are the reduced row and fixed squarefree label on that side. A residual puncture at $g/j$ has cost at most $\log_U(G/J)$, even if the fixed exclusions have already supplied some of those zeros. This is the sharper puncture bound used by the hybrid estimate. The $v$ count is taken once after the pointwise-in-$v$ Hilbert bound. Both frequency conditions depend only on the bare $h$ and can be imposed before the signed rational coprimality inversion; their overlap is removed once.

The local tests in the [sixth-power estimate](long-inverse-frequency-sectors.md) and the [grouped conductor sieve](long-inverse-grouped-sieve.md) use $r_B,s_B$. Here $r_B=r-(s-s_B)>1$ and $s_B\le s$. The original large-sixth-power gate $\log_U Nv\ge 2r/67+s$ implies its local counterpart. The grouped condition $5\xi-12\nu\le r-28s$ also implies the local condition because $r_B-28s_B=r-28s+27(s-s_B)$. Thus the cutoff $NE(h)\le U^{.199995r}$ holds at the original exponent on every retained projected scale. For the more general sixth-power hybrid gates the right sides also decrease when replacing $r,s$ by $r_B,s_B$. The tests used for Sobolev and the compactly supported Fourier comparison have the same uniform seminorm and height controls. This proves the simultaneous conductor and frequency restrictions on this sufficient version.
