# The divisor price for two fixed ordinary rays

Assume the preprint’s $7/8$ theorem and the native #143 source hypotheses. Fix the number of marked coordinates and $\epsilon>0$. For squarefree $D$, each signed allocation $a$ of its prime divisors, and an incoming ordinary prefix $d=(d_1,d_2)$, the squared radial energy of the decorated allocated row is at most
$$
 C_\epsilon U^\epsilon\,\frac{E}{ND\,N(d_1d_2)}.
$$
Here $E$ bounds the energies of the canonical separated leaves on this same radial measure, with the original two fixed ordinary multipliers and original marked coefficients on a surviving subset. Each leaf’s two lengths are componentwise below either $(X_1,X_2)$ or $(Y_1,Y_2)$. The argument keeps the ordered pair of lengths. This is the input on the short source in the checked label theorem. [Allocated price transfer](lean/AsymmetricRayLabel.lean)

Use the native finite prime enclosure in which each prime has norm bounded by a fixed power of $U$, the two supported ordinary profiles with $X_1X_2=Y_1Y_2$, and marked prime pools with $1\le P_i$ and $Np\le 2P_i$. The marked weights are uniformly bounded on these pools, with the stated bounded number of holes per slot. Write $R$ for the puncture entering the allocated row, including the previously extracted genuine common factor. Its common deleted primes, those over $D$, and the ordinary punctures $H$ have combined radical norm at most $U^{B_{\rm mask}}$ with fixed $B_{\rm mask}$. Every prime of $H$ is already deleted from each marked pool. Row weights are nonnegative and have compact radial support. The moving character is the native natural character on kept, nonzero rows. Use two fixed ordinary ray characters with their multiplicative extensions by zero. Their ideal coefficients have modulus at most one.

## Extraction

For each coordinate $v_j$ of a nonzero tuple take its entire $D$-primary part $B_j$ and put $u_j=v_j/B_j$. The signed allocation specifies a **nonempty subset** of coordinates for each $p\mid D$. Its term vanishes unless $p$ divides every selected $B_j$. Thus $D\mid\prod_j B_j$ on every contributing label. All remaining $u_j$ are coprime to $D$. The cube-free tuple restriction bounds $B_j\mid D^4$ (the native loose bound suffices). With $k$ prime slots there are at most $5^{(k+2)\omega(D)}$ labels. For any fixed $A,\eta>0$, $A^{\omega(D)}\ll_{A,\eta}(ND)^\eta$ by separating the finitely many prime ideals with $Np<A^{1/\eta}$. Since $D$ is squarefree, its norm has the assumed polynomial bound. The same estimate pays for taking the square of the label count. [Signed allocation and scalar budget][budget]

The two ordinary rays split into their factors at $B_0,B_1$ and $u_0,u_1$, where subscripts $0,1$ now denote the ordinary coordinates. The frozen factor includes the signed allocation, the original frozen restriction and the actual moving character of $\prod_j B_j$. Its modulus is at most one. A nontrivial marked $B_i$ fixes the entire prime $v_i=B_i$ and contributes squared normalized weight $O(1/P_i)\ll 1/NB_i$. An unfrozen marked coordinate keeps precisely its original relative character, profile and height. The surviving tuple has the native cube-free, coprimality and distinct-prime restrictions away from $D$. Its common puncture is $\operatorname{rad}(RD)$, and its ordinary punctures $H$ remain. This is an exact sum decomposition before the square, valid also when a ray or a row character vanishes. [Decorated allocated row identity](lean/AsymmetricRayColumn.lean)

In each of the two arms of the ordinary profile the lengths become $X_j/N(d_jB_j)$ or $Y_j/N(d_jB_j)$. Changing from the original square-root normalization to the shortened one contributes $1/\sqrt{N(d_1d_2B_0B_1)}$ in amplitude. The equal products make this factor identical on both arms. The total squared cost for one active label is therefore
$$
 \ll_k \frac{1}{N(d_1d_2)\prod_j NB_j}
 \le \frac{C_k}{N(d_1d_2)\,ND}.
$$
The factor two from separating the centered difference is constant. Cauchy over the finite labels costs at most the square of their count against a common upper bound for the residual energies. It remains to deduce such a bound from $E$. [Frozen normalization][normalization]

## From a residual arm to separated leaves

Put $T=\operatorname{supp}(RD)\cup H$ and give the moving character its actual zeros at $T$. This implements the tuple’s common mask. Enlarging the ordinary puncture to a marked puncture at $H$ is exact because the marked coefficients already vanish there. Complete the finite supported rectangle using the native enclosure. On kept rows every unsupported ideal already has moving coefficient zero. Multiplication by either ray preserves that zero. On this rectangle apply the decorated forward identity with `inv=false`. Its source is the restricted tuple and its child ordinary kernel is identically one. The frozen divisor pair $e$ multiplies its original scalar by the phase $\omega_0(e_0)\omega_1(e_1)$ and divides each ordinary length by its respective $Ne_j$. The positive scalar estimate in `subpower_energy` costs $U^\eta$ for arbitrarily small fixed $\eta>0$, since each prime in the enclosure has polynomially bounded norm. It leaves a product of the two punctured ordinary polynomials times an injective natural prime sum. The finitely many frozen slots add at most $k$ holes per surviving slot. [Forward identity and positive estimate](lean/AsymmetricRayKernel.lean) [Frozen-slot dictionary][forward]

Apply `NativeInjectiveEnergy.natural_radial_energy` to this product. Its ordinary multiplier is an arbitrary function of the row, so one may insert exactly the two decorated punctured polynomials. Its prime slot bound holds because the moving character with its additional zeros is bounded by one and the original slot has size $O((Np)^{-1/2})$. This costs another $U^\eta$ and leaves products of ordinary polynomials and unrestricted prime sums on subsets, with at most $k^2$ new holes per surviving slot. [Collision estimate][collision]

Finally open the common puncture separately in each ordinary polynomial by Möbius inversion over divisors $s_j\mid\prod_{p\in T}p$. Replacing the ordinary ideal by $s_j l_j$ gives the extra coefficient $\mu(s_j)(\omega_j\chi_z)(s_j)/\sqrt{Ns_j}$ using the moving character **before** deleting $T$. Its modulus is at most one. The new length is divided by $Ns_j$ and its tilt remains in the normalized profile. In each prime sum open the deletion as the full original sum minus the individual excluded prime terms. There are $O(1+|T|)$ choices per slot, each excluded term being bounded since $P_i\ge1$. The two ordinary expansions together have at most $4^{|T|}$ terms. Their squared counts and the prime choices cost $U^\eta$ for any prescribed $\eta>0$ by the radical bound. The remaining factors are the canonical separated leaves defining $E$, with the original moving character and both fixed ordinary rays. Every subsequent divisor has norm at least one. Taking the finitely many intermediate exponents small enough gives the stated $U^\epsilon$. [Native puncture and hole removal][holes]

## Lean coverage

`allocated_to_source` proves the divisor saving for a fixed signed allocation. Its hypothesis bounds each active residual source by its updated ordinary pair norm. Its conclusion has the additional factor $(ND)^{\delta}/ND$, for every $\delta>0$, with an explicit profile factor. `source_centered` identifies each residual source with the difference of the two individually shortened arms, and `absorb` moves the ordinary holes into the character under the native vanishing condition on the marked coefficients.
