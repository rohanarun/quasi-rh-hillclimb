# Zeros of the physical denominator near the Mellin coordinate

Assume the preprint’s $7/8$ theorem and the physical and marked estimates of tracker #143. On the critical patch put $U=Z^D$, $.815<D<.815325$, $a=(1+\delta)/2$, $\eta=10^{-7}$ and $\Delta_{\max}=6.363\cdot10^{-8}$. The actual denominator is $f_u(s)=L_{\rm orig}(s,\eta_{\rm ray}\overline{\chi_\bullet(u)})$. After contributions saving the target $3\eta/4$ in the physical integral are discarded, the following restrictions are available.

- On the original physical line there is a zero $\rho$ of this denominator with $\Re\rho>a-\Delta_{\max}$ and $|\Im\rho-\Im s|<4.2\cdot10^{-6}$. A disk of radius $.000025$ about this very zero contains at least $10^{-200}(.000025)\log U$ zeros of the same primitive character, with multiplicity.
- One may instead retain both the long inverse dyad of the physical denominator and such a zero with $|\Im\rho-\Im s|<2.401$, with the same cluster bound. The signed detector can use this zero even when its real part is slightly below $a$.
- A uniform signed estimate at exponent $R-1.381\eta$, required only for this denominator on the original amplitude class, suffices for the target physical saving. Here $R$ is the original class-count exponent. This arithmetic estimate remains open.

The first two statements restrict contributions to the physical integral. The inverse-gap exception used for the cluster has the separate row count given below. [Original physical denominator and inverse dyad](physical-denominator.md) · [Signed cross term](signed-detector-cross-term.md)

## Cluster on the physical denominator at its physical zero

The inverse-gap exception has $O(U^{.61525+\epsilon})$ original rows, which the physical fourth moment handles at the required price. On the remaining rows the interval $.91\le r\le.97$ of normalized inverse dyads saves $.001$ from its buffered pointwise exponent. We apply the one-residue Gaussian test at the physical zero $\rho=\sigma+i\gamma$ with $a-\Delta_{\max}\le\sigma<a+2e$. Its fixed parameters are $d_*=.000025$ and

$$T=\log U,\quad y=.94,\quad K_*=1700,\quad H_*=d_*/4=6.25\cdot10^{-6},\quad D_*=6.5\cdot10^{-8},\quad c=10^{-200}.$$

Suppose the $d_*$ disk contains $N<cd_*T$ zeros. Write $w=(s-\rho)/d_*$ and let $w_j$ be their coordinates with $w_1=0$, repeating multiplicities. Set $F(w)=f_u(\rho+d_*w)/\prod_j(w-w_j)$. Locally $|f_u|\le U^{d_*}$ since $.51(d_*+\Delta_{\max}+6e)<.52d_*$. For each rescaled zero $w_j$, $\Re w_j\le(\Delta_{\max}+2e)/d_*<.003$. The disk $|w-1/4|\le1/8$ is within the original right buffer, and all its distances from $w_j$ are in $[.1,2]$. Thus the same Blaschke removal, Borel–Carathéodory and three-circles proof in the selected-zero argument give $|\log F|/T\le43d_*(23c)^{1/16}<D_*/100$ on $|w-1/4|\le3/8$. The contour rectangle of half-height $H_*$ and half-width $D_*$ stays inside, since $(1/4+D_*/d_*)^2+(1/4)^2<(3/8)^2$. [Local pole removal](selected-zero-cluster.md#removing-all-local-poles-except-one)

The test on the initial absolute line is
$$ I=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{\Re z=2-\sigma}
  \frac{P(z)}{f_u(\rho+z)}\exp\big(T(yz+K_*z^2)\big)\,dz,$$
where $P(z)=\prod_{j=2}^{N}(z/d_*-w_j)$.

Move the initial contour to $\Re z=D_*$, where $\sigma+D_*>a+6e$, inside the tall buffered rectangle, with negligible joins, before cutting it to $|\Im z|\le H_*$. During the initial tail deletion use $c|t|\le .01K_*t^2+25c^2/K_*$. The outer tail exponent, up to arbitrarily small losses, is $yD_*+K_*D_*^2-.99K_*H_*^2<-4.6\cdot10^{-9}$, since the extra term $cd_*\log(1+D_*/d_*)+25c^2/K_*$ is negligible. Move the remaining segment to $\Re z=-D_*$. Its local horizontal joins use $yD_*+K_*D_*^2-K_*H_*^2+D_*/100<-4.6\cdot10^{-9}$, and the left segment costs at most $-yD_*+K_*D_*^2+D_*/100<-5.9\cdot10^{-8}$. All three gains exceed $D_*/100$. Since $P(z)/f_u(\rho+z)=d_*/(zF(z/d_*))$, the one remaining residue gives $|I|\ge(d_*/2)U^{-D_*/100}$.

Expand the original absolute-line integral in inverse coefficients and move the entire kernel, dyad by dyad, to the common saddle $(r-y)/(2K_*)$ at dyadic exponent $r$. Its scalar modulus after integration is bounded by a fixed multiple of $U^{-(r-y)^2/(8K_*)+c^2/K_*}$ in addition to the factor $U^{(1/2-\sigma)r}$. On $.91\le r\le .97$ the new drift only costs $U^{.97\Delta_{\max}}$ against the saved $U^{-.001}$. For every $r\ge0$ outside that window one has

$$\frac{(r-y)^2}{8K_*}-\Delta_{\max}r\ \ge\ \frac{(.03)^2}{8K_*}-.97\Delta_{\max}>4.4\cdot10^{-9}.$$

Indeed the quadratic is increasing for $r\ge.97$ and decreasing throughout $[0,.91]$, and the value at $.91$ exceeds that at $.97$. ## A uniform signed price needed only on the physical denominator

Take $g=1.381\eta$. For the actual physical denominator and the original buffered amplitude class $\mathcal C$ at fixed external data, suppose
$$
 -\Re\sum_{u\in\mathcal C}(A_j\overline{B_j})_{\rm nonprincipal}(Z_{\rm tot};\sigma+it)
 \ll_\epsilon U^{R-g+\epsilon}Z_{\rm tot}^{\delta}(1+H_{\rm ext}+|t|)^J .
$$
Use the three original log-weight tests $j=0,1,2$, uniformly on the detector’s total scales, on $a-\Delta_{\max}\le\sigma\le a+2e$ and on all allowed common heights. Here $H_{\rm ext}$ is the external height and $J$ is fixed. The known sectors meeting this price can also be removed. If $0\le v\le\Delta_{\max}$, the number of original rows in the class having a physical denominator zero with $\Re\rho\ge a-v$ anywhere in the eligible buffered height interval is

$$\ll U^{R-\max(0,g-2L v)+\epsilon},\qquad L=1.12906.$$

The bin gives the upper zero guard $\Re\rho<a+2e$. On the rows surviving the uniform single-factor exclusions, run the unit-calibrated signed detector at that physical zero with source cutoff $U^{t_*-5\lambda}$. For the Gamma calibration, use its pre-loss excluded-factor margin $1.5\eta$ and take the small errors below its fixed slack. At ordinary length $y'\le.51$ the extra cost to restore $Z_{\rm tot}^{1/2-\sigma}$ is at most $1.65\Delta_{\max}<1.06\eta$ for sufficiently large $U$. At $y'>.51$, the ordinary reflected exponent gains $2(a-1/2)(y'-.5)$, more than the drift $\Delta_{\max}(t_*+y'+o(1))$ for the whole bounded length range. Thus the detector gives $|A|\gg Z_{\rm tot}^{a-1/2} U^{-Lv}/\log U$ at some total center $Z_{\rm tot}=U^\ell$, with $\ell\le t_*+2\lambda+o(1)<L$, using $t_*<1.12903$, $\lambda=10^{-5}$. The exact full-convolution error $|A+B|\ll Z_{\rm tot}^{a-1/2}U^{-\eta}$ is smaller for every such $v$ since $L\Delta_{\max}<.72\eta$. The bound derived from the signed estimate by $|A_j|^2\le |A_j+B_j|^2-2\Re(A_j\overline{B_j})$, two-variable Sobolev, and Markov works on the fixed real interval with the extended lower endpoint. It uses $g<2\eta$ for the error square. All row-dependent zero choices and ordinate intervals can be imposed after this positive squared estimate. The exceptions to the single-factor bounds have $O(U^{R-3\eta})$ members and can be reinserted. Finally cap by the original $O(U^{R+\epsilon})$ row count. Except for the bin starting at zero, no zero in the height window has real part greater than $a-v_i$. For the contour on this bin take $l_i=\max(0,v_i-\omega)$.
