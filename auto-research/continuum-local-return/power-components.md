# Quadratic and cubic components of the remaining dual frequencies

Assume the preprint's 7/8 theorem and the physical inputs of [the continuum return](../proposals/2026-11-17-local-continuum-return.md). In the [paired Poisson expansion](column-correlations.md) write a nonzero dual ideal $h$ in three ways,
$$
 h=c_2 t_2^2=c_3 t_3^3=c_6 t_6^6,
$$
where $c_i$ is $i$-th-power-free. The radical of $c_2$ records the primes where the valuation of $h$ is odd. The radical of $c_3$ records the primes where that valuation is nonzero modulo three. These are the ramified good primes of the quadratic and cubic powers, respectively, of the sextic residue character attached to $h$. Fixed ray factors can affect the primes above six. The estimates below use ideal factorizations and include those primes in the cutoffs.

Let $e(h)$ be the product of primes whose valuation in $h$ is exactly one. After the common-divisor split below, the required price holds whenever **any one** of the four indicated bounds holds. Entries are upper bounds on $\log_U$ of the indicated norms. In particular this covers every powerful ideal, meaning every prime in it occurs at least twice, including every square and cube.

| Original column | $Ne(h)$ | $N c_2$ | $N\operatorname{rad}(c_3)$ | $N\operatorname{rad}(c_6)$ | Required price |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Mixed inverse and ordinary | $2/25$ | $1/40$ | $2/25$ | $1/4$ | $553/500$ |
| Two inverses | $7/500$ | $1/10$ | $1/5$ | $749/1000$ | $63/50$ |

In the mixed case the valuation-one bound already includes both lower-order bounds, since $e(h)$ divides $c_2$ and $\operatorname{rad}(c_3)$. For two inverses the component bounds also apply to frequencies whose valuation-one part exceeds $U^{7/500}$. Large prime ideals outside the displayed cutoffs remain among the untreated frequencies.

## The quadratic and cubic components

For a fixed $g,j,r$ use the same-ray paired Poisson formula and put $G=X+Y+(L')^{2/3}$, $H=U^\epsilon(L')^2/M$. The rapidly decaying tail beyond $H$ can be made smaller than any prescribed negative power. Invert the coprimality of the two residual columns with $d\mid(a,a')$. Before the prefactor $M/L'$, each separated one-sided column has squared coefficient norm $O_\epsilon(U^\epsilon/(Ng\,Nd))$ and squared magnitude at each frequency at most $O_\epsilon(U^\epsilon G/(Ng\,Nd))$. The first follows by counting the multiples of $gd$ with $|\lambda_n|\ll U^\epsilon L^{-1/2}$. The second is the cubic Gauss-correlation bound of [the preceding proof](column-correlations.md). Mellin separation leaves fixed coefficients across each dual row sum. Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the sum of absolute values of the two-sided products by the two one-sided squared norms. In the next calculations suppress the common $U^\epsilon/(Ng\,Nd)$.

**The square component.** Fix $c=c_2$ and write $t_2=v^3 a$ with $a$ cubefree. The part of the row character depending on $a$ is the cubic symbol $J(a,n)^{\pm2}$, with its original zeros. The entire $v$-factor is a column exclusion at the primes of $v$. The factor at $c$ is a fixed twist. These assertions hold with shared primes among $a,c,v$. For $Nv\asymp B$ let $T=(H/Nc)^{1/2}$ and $A\asymp T/B^3$. There are $O(B)$ choices of $v$. At each one, de Faveri's cubic large sieve allows all cubefree $a$ and bounds the squared sum by $O_\epsilon(U^\epsilon[A+L'+(AL')^{2/3}])$ after the normalization just specified. The row-independent column moduli may be restricted to squarefree ideals. Conjugation handles the opposite orientation. A fixed part of $a$ supported on $S$ has only boundedly many choices and gives another bounded column twist. If a factor at the extracted divisor $d$ still depends on $a$, its modulus is at most one and it can be discarded in each positive square. [De Faveri, Theorem 1.1 and the cubic family in Example 2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04045v1#S2.SS4).

The individual-frequency bound gives $O(AG)$ at the same $v$. For a nonempty range $A\gg1$. Summing the better bound on dyads of $v$ costs at most
$$
 U^\epsilon\left(T+(TL')^{2/3}+(L')^{2/3}(TG)^{1/3}\right),
$$
since $B A\ll T$, $B(AL')^{2/3}\ll(TL')^{2/3}$ and $\min(BL',TG/B^2)\le(L')^{2/3}(TG)^{1/3}$. Multiply by $M/L'$, reinstate the coefficient normalization, and sum over $Nc\le V$ by ideal counting. The resulting bound, up to $U^\epsilon/(Ng\,Nd)$, is
$$
 M^{1/2}V^{1/2}+M^{2/3}(L')^{1/3}V^{2/3}+M^{5/6}G^{1/3}V^{5/6}.
$$

**The cube component.** Fix $c=c_3$ and write $t_3=v^2 a$ with $a$ squarefree. Now the varying character is the quadratic symbol $J(a,n)^{\pm3}$ and $v$ again contributes its full coprimality mask. With $T=(H/Nc)^{1/3}$ and $A\asymp T/B^2$, the quadratic large sieve gives $O_\epsilon(U^\epsilon[A+L'])$ and the individual-frequency estimate gives $O(AG)$. The fixed bad-prime and orientation conventions are as above. The resulting dyadic bound is $O_\epsilon(U^\epsilon[T+\sqrt{TL'G}])$, using $\min(BL',TG/B)\le\sqrt{TL'G}$. For each squarefree $e=\operatorname{rad}(c)$ there are at most $2^{\omega(e)}$ possible $c$ and $Nc\ge Ne$. On multiplying by $M/L'$ and summing through $Ne\le V$, the cost up to the same normalization is
$$
 M^{2/3}(L')^{-1/3} V^{2/3}
 +M^{5/6}(L')^{-1/6}\sqrt G\,V^{5/6}.
$$
[Quadratic sieve, equation (1.4)](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04045v1#S1.SS1).

For each displayed term the power $M^a$ has $a\ge1/2$. The sum over $j$ therefore costs at most a harmonic sum up to the physical cutoff $Nj\ll\sqrt K$. The sums over $g,d$ with weights $1/(Ng\,Nd)$ and the divisors $r$ cost at most another $U^\epsilon$. To check the cube bound uniformly use
$$
 (L')^{-1/6}\sqrt G\ll
 \max\big(\max(X,Y)^{1/2}U^{-b/6},\,L^{1/6}\big).
$$
Substituting the actual length enclosures and the table's cutoffs gives strict margins in every case. The [rational replay](four_factors.py) checks those powers and the squared comparison that bounds $E(b)$. Each sector estimate permits arbitrary subcollections of the indicated frequencies after summing the column indices and taking absolute values in the frequency. The union bound is therefore valid for overlapping sectors.

## The product of the primes of valuation one

Write $c=e(h)=\prod_{\operatorname{ord}_p(h)=1}p$. The estimates also cover all frequencies with $Nc\le U^{2/25}$ for a mixed pair and $Nc\le U^{7/500}$ for two inverses. In particular they cover every powerful $h$, for which $c=1$. Keep the preceding common-divisor split, the prefactor $M/L'$, and the one-sided coefficient and individual-frequency bounds. All three sieves below act on positive squares after Cauchy–Schwarz in the frequency.

Write the frequency uniquely as
$$
h=c\,a^2b^3v^6,
\qquad a\ \text{cubefree},\quad b\ \text{squarefree},\quad (c,abv)=1.
$$
For a prime outside $c$, the six possible exponents $2\operatorname{ord}_p(a)+3\operatorname{ord}_p(b)$ are $0,2,3,4,5,7$, distinct modulo six. Choose the corresponding exponent, then put the remaining multiple of six into $v^6$. In residue class one, a positive valuation outside $c$ is at least seven. This proves the factorization and permits shared primes among $a,b,v$. At fixed $v$, its entire character factor is the column mask $1_{(v,n)=1}$. Split off the parts of $c,a,b$ supported on the fixed set $S$. Their exponents are bounded, so this costs only a fixed number of twists. Choose literal generators for these fixed parts. The paired character is unit-trivial, and within each primary column class an additional unit factor is a single scalar.

**The classical character sieve.** For the good parts at fixed $c$, sextic reciprocity on fixed ray classes converts the varying phase to the residue character $n\mapsto(n/(a^2b^3))_6$ or its conjugate, up to a fixed phase. Its primitive modulus is $\mathfrak q=\operatorname{rad}(ab)$, with $N\mathfrak q\le Na\,Nb$. At each good prime of this modulus the residue character has exact order six before raising to its indicated nonzero exponent modulo six. Hence the character is nontrivial there, and the original zero extension agrees with extension by zero at the primitive modulus. The local exponents recover $a$ and $b$ even when they share primes. Alternatively, let $c$ vary in the same sieve. The modulus becomes $\operatorname{rad}(cab)$, of norm at most $Nc\,Na\,Nb$. There are at most $2^{\omega(\mathfrak q)}$ triples giving any one primitive character. Only a local exponent one has two possible assignments, to $c$ or to $a^2b^3$ with exponent seven. The fixed ray subdivisions and this divisor multiplicity cost $O_\epsilon(U^\epsilon)$. A scalar from an already extracted column divisor can depend on the row. Its modulus is at most one in the positive square. All remaining column masks and smooth factors are independent of the rows averaged by the chosen sieve. [Sextic reciprocity and unit conventions](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Quasi-Riemann-Hypothesis-September-30-2026/build/paper.tex#L4781-L4834).

For completeness, the primitive residue-character sieve on the Eisenstein integers at modulus norm $\le D$ and column norm $\ll L'$ costs $O(D^2+L')$ times the squared coefficient norm. Represent the column ideals by their unique primary elements and extend the coefficients by zero on the rest of the lattice. Write each good modulus as $(q)$ with primary generator $q$. For a primitive residue character the finite Gauss transform has magnitude $\sqrt{Nq}$ at a unit frequency and vanishes at nonunits. Expanding the character sum and applying orthogonality on $(\mathcal O/q)^\times$ therefore gives
$$
\sum_{\chi\ {\rm primitive}\bmod q}
 \left|\sum_n B_n\chi(n)\right|^2
 \le \frac{\varphi(q)}{Nq}
      \sum_{r\bmod q}^{*}\left|\sum_n B_n e(nr/q)\right|^2.
$$
The reduced fractions $r/q$ for these primary moduli are distinct modulo the Eisenstein lattice and have separation $\gg D^{-1}$. Indeed, after subtracting any lattice translate, the cross numerator of two distinct fractions is a nonzero Eisenstein integer, and $|qq'|\le D$. The fixed self-dual trace pairing gives the same spacing up to a constant. To see the planar additive sieve, choose a nonnegative Schwartz function on the plane that is bounded below by one on the unit disk and has compactly supported Fourier transform. A suitably rescaled squared modulus of the Fourier transform of a smooth compactly supported function of nonzero integral has these properties. In the dual squared norm majorize the lattice sum by this function at scale $T=C(\sqrt{L'}+D)$. With a sufficiently large fixed $C$, Poisson summation and the separation leave only the diagonal frequencies, at cost $O(T^2)$. Duality gives the asserted bound. The unit modulus contributes at most $O(L')$ by Cauchy–Schwarz. [Ordinary character large sieve, Lemma 2.2](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/An-unconditional-first-moment-for-cubic-Gauss-sums-September-25-2026/build/sections/background-type1.tex#L175-L232).

**Summing the dyadic ranges.** Write $U^z,U^A,U^B,U^\nu$ for the dyadic norm scales of $c,a,b,v$, respectively. Put $\ell=\log_U L'$ and $m=\log_U M$. Use nonnegative exponents for the dyads containing the unit. Bounded endpoints of $M$ can likewise be clipped to $[1,U]$ with fixed constant factors, so $0\le m\le1$. The frequency cutoff $H=U^\epsilon(L')^2/M$ imposes
$$
 z+2A+3B+6\nu \le 2\ell-m+O(\epsilon).
$$
Write $e=\max(x,y,2\ell/3)$, so $G\ll U^e$. Fix $g,d,r$, with $r\mid\operatorname{rad}(gw\prod_{p\in S}p)$. For any dyad of $M=K/[(Nj)^2 Nr]$ there are $O(U^{(1-m)/2})$ eligible ideals $j$, since $K\ll U$ and $Nr\ge1$. We use the sieve per fixed $j$, so its phases can still depend on $j$. The number of ideals $v$ on their dyad is $O(U^\nu)$. After including $M/L'$ the common exponent, apart from the price normalized by $L'$, is therefore $P=(1+m)/2+\nu$. In each comparison below add $O(\epsilon)$ for the truncation and dyadic endpoints.

The individual-frequency bound adds $z+A+B+e-\ell$ to $P$. The cubic sieve on $a$ for fixed $c,b,v$ adds $z+B+(2A-\ell)_+/3$. Put $S_0=A+B$. The same three bounds suffice for both columns.

If $2A\le\ell$ and $2S_0\ge\ell$, take the average of the cubic addition $z+B$ and the fixed-$c$ classical addition $z+2S_0-\ell$. The same upper bound on $P$ gives $(1+\ell+z)/2$ again.

3. When $u\ge z$ the joint classical addition is zero. The individual-frequency addition is $t=z-u+e-\ell/2$. Using $\min(0,t)\le t/3$ with the same bound on $P$ gives a total at most $5/6+e/3+z/6$.

## Why multiplicativity alone cannot bound the remaining moment

There is a single completely multiplicative function $f$ with values in $\{1,-1\}$ for which allowing $f$ as an additional common twist destroys the proposed complete mixed moment bound along an unbounded sequence of scales. This example keeps the Möbius factor and the ordinary factor, and uses fixed smooth bumps. The admissible arithmetic twists in the physical problem are specified Hecke presentations.

For the construction take nonnegative bumps supported on $[1,2]$, bounded below on $[1.7,1.8]$, and norm heights zero. Take admissible mixed lengths with $X/Y\to\infty$. Fix a good prime row $k_0$ of norm in $[U,2U]$. In each short interval $[1.7X,1.8X]$ and $[1.7Y,1.8Y]$ retain primes in the most populous joint class for $J(k_0,p)$ and the fixed primary ray. The prime ideal theorem and finite pigeonholing give sets $A,B$ of sizes $\gg X/\log X,Y/\log Y$. The finitely many excluded primes can be omitted. The two intervals are disjoint and every selected prime has norm below $Nk_0$ for large $U$.

On $A$ choose one common sign $t$ for $f$ and on $B$ choose one common sign $v$. Fix its values at every other relevant prime. Average $t v P_{k_0}$ over the four choices of signs.
