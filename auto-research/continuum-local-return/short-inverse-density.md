# A complete eighteenth-moment bound at the physical length

Assume the preprint's $7/8$ theorem and its unmarked inverse and ordinary moment estimates used in tracker #143. On the complete Eisenstein element shell $Nu\asymp U$ consider a fixed finite product of centrally normalized smooth inverse polynomials of a common fixed admissible presentation, with possibly distinct fixed norm heights and scales $Y_j\ge1$, and write $L=\sum_j\log_U Y_j$. Allow original Euler omissions and extra coprimality masks at ideals of polynomial norm, common or individual in each factor. All source heights, including a height supremum if needed, carry the original fixed polynomial costs; when absorbed into $U^\epsilon$ their fixed positive height power is chosen afterwards. For $0\le L\le.8883$, the squared moment is
$$
 \ll_\epsilon U^{1+J(L)+\epsilon},\qquad
 J(L)=\begin{cases}0&L\le2/3,\\
 (\sqrt{15L}-\sqrt{10})^2/6&L>2/3.
 \end{cases}                                                     \tag{1}
$$
In particular the twelfth moment of the genuine short inverse at $z<.0987$ is $O(U^{1+\epsilon})$, and its eighteenth moment is $O(U^{1.03970+\epsilon})$. The single physical Möbius polynomial admits a sharper eighteenth moment using a prime sum forced by that polynomial. The remaining target is $1.00029$. ### The unmarked dynamic count

First work at a sixth-power-free primitive row scale $V$. For the present inverse-only application take the floor $1/2+\epsilon_f$ with arbitrarily small fixed $\epsilon_f>0$. In the buffered bin lemma, replace $51/100$ by this floor both in the maxima and their rounding. The finite pigeonhole proof is identical, the controlling disks still have real part greater than $1/2$, and the preprint's logarithmic-control lemma permits $a\in[1/2,1]$. In a nonfloor bin a zero with $\Re\rho\ge a$ exists in the required height window. Use its same original presentation family (including conjugates); fixed inducing exceptions are bounded at sixth-power-free scale. In the floor bin every inverse has the pointwise squared estimate $Y_j^{2\epsilon_f}V^\epsilon$ from buffered Mellin inversion. This step is only for the independent moment in (1). It does not change any original physical contour or its floor.

On a nonfloor bin put $\delta=2a-1$. The $7/8$ theorem gives $0<\delta\le3/4$. The original Gamma detector argument at any $1\le t\le3/2$ still gives two saturated witnesses of exponents $r,m$ at base $V$ with $r\le t+o(1)$ and $r+m\ge t-o(1)$. In that proof $\Re(\rho-1/4)\ge1/4$ suffices for the deleted Euler bounds, and $-3+t(5/4-\Re\rho)\le-15/8$. The division by $\delta$ in controlling $m-1/2$ uses $\delta\ge2\epsilon_f$, so the resulting constants may depend on this fixed floor. All power losses can be decreased after choosing $\epsilon_f$. ### All element rows and the power comparison

Write $(u)=v^6(u_0)$ with $(u_0)$ sixth-power-free, fixing generators and the finitely many units. At primes dividing $v$ the polynomial on $u$ is the polynomial of $u_0$ with those primes omitted. For places in the originally excluded fixed set, freeze the valuations of the sixth-power-free part (at most five), absorb their fixed Kummer phase in a fixed twist of the row presentation, and use the finite list of resulting fixed data. Any additional omitted primes of polynomial total norm cost $U^\epsilon$ in the pointwise estimate. Indeed inverse Euler factors at $\Re s\ge1/2$ cost $O_\epsilon((N\mathfrak b)^\epsilon)$ for a puncture $\mathfrak b$, and no new zero in the right half-plane is introduced. The dynamic count is taken for the underlying original presentation family before these extra punctures.

Freeze dyads $Nv\asymp U^\nu$, $V=Nu_0\asymp U^{1-6\nu}$, ignoring fixed endpoint constants. There are $O(U^\nu)$ choices for $v$. If $V\le U^{1/100}$, the number of all such rows is $O(U^{1/6+(5/6)/100+\epsilon})$, and the trivial squared bound $U^{L+\epsilon}$ alone would suffice for $L\le2/3$. For the full stated range use the global $7/8$ zero-free bound: move the inverse dyad's Mellin line to $7/8+e$ globally by the preprint's logarithmic control and the deleted Euler bounds, obtaining squared product $\ll U^{(3/4)L+\epsilon}$. This applies also to the principal reciprocal by its source regularization. Thus these rows cost at most $U^{1/6+1/120+(3/4)(.8883)+\epsilon}<U^{1+\epsilon}$. The global bound holds uniformly for the same bounded or slowly growing norm heights.

For $V>U^{1/100}$ all exponents $\log_VY_j$ are bounded by a fixed constant. Choose the external height power sufficiently small relative to this fixed threshold for the buffered pointwise estimates, the detector, and the unmarked moments. The sixth-power-free families permit the fixed punctures and the source's finite inducing exceptions are absorbed in the small-scale case. The floor's total cost is at most $U^{1+2\epsilon_f L+\epsilon}$ by direct row counting. On a nonfloor bin the pointwise squared product is at most $U^{\delta L+\epsilon}$. Multiplication with the sixth-power-free count and the number of $v$ gives exponent
$$
 \nu+(1-6\nu)(1-\delta C(\delta))+\delta L
 =1+\delta(L-C(\delta))+\nu(-5+6\delta C(\delta)).
$$
The last coefficient is negative since $\delta C(\delta)\le5/7$. For $L\le2/3$ the remaining excess is nonpositive. For $L>2/3$ set $x=15-6\delta>0$ and apply the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality:
$$
 \delta(L-C(\delta))
 =\frac{15L+10-Lx-150/x}{6}
 \le \frac{(\sqrt{15L}-\sqrt{10})^2}{6}.
$$
The dyadic and finite bin choices, floor width and preliminary losses fit into any prescribed $U^\epsilon$. This proves (1). The height parameters and bumps may vary rowwise in the buffered pointwise product, subject to the uniform source conditions.

### Limit of the unmarked density calculation

Changing the moment order in (1) cannot supply the missing physical count. For any total width $L$ covered by (1), use the lower bound $U^{\delta L}$ on the squared product of saturated factors, even granting zero loss in that bound. Markov's inequality with (1) gives exponent $1+J(L)-\delta L$. The definition of $J$ and the arithmetic-geometric mean calculation above give
$$
 1+J(L)-\delta L\ge 1-\delta C(\delta).
$$
The original critical count has exponent $R=1-\delta C_{\rm phys}$, where $C_{\rm phys}=p(1+2g_0)+(1-p(1+2g_1))2t_0/3$. On the [physical rectangle](short-physical-inverse.md) its lower bound from $t_0>1.12796$ is $.876842854921$, whereas $C(\delta)\le 10/(15-6(.4267))$. Using $\delta\ge.425$ therefore gives $1-\delta C(\delta)>R+.031$ throughout the rectangle. This lower bound concerns the exponent furnished by the density calculation, for any number of saturated factors in its range. The arithmetic correlation of the actual factors could give a stronger estimate.

### Dense pairs with a common rational norm divisor

For the original ninth-power column $C$, the signed sector with $\gcd_{\mathbb Z}(NC,NC')\ge U^{.2040}$ costs $O_\epsilon(U^{1.000288+\epsilon})$ in the complete smooth all-element square. The assertion also holds if all columns are required to have rational-squarefree norm (and exclude the finitely many additional rational primes). Take $P_k$ to be the subpolynomial on $k\mid NC$. The mixed differences of each rational-prime threshold applied to the nine ideal-coordinate valuations express this as a sum of $\tau(k)^{O(1)}$ coordinate divisibilities $d_j\mid n_j$ with $D=\prod Nd_j\ge k$. At a split prime use the threshold on the sum of the eighteen nonnegative valuations, whose mixed differences are supported on total exponent between $e$ and $e+17$ when $p^e\Vert k$. At inert primes each ideal valuation counts twice for rational norm, so the threshold is $\lceil e/2\rceil$ on their sum. The sum of absolute coefficients is a fixed power of $\tau(k)$. In a nonempty term each $d_j$ is squarefree as an ideal. Extract $d_j$, retaining its row phase, the coprimality of the remaining inverse variable to $d_j$, and the normalization $D^{-1/2}$. Products with $k\ge U^{.2040}$ have remaining total exponent at most $9(.0987)-.2040=.6843+O(1/\log U)$. If any scale is below one but has support, rescale to one at fixed dilation. Formula (1) now gives
$$
 \|P_k\|_2\ll_\epsilon U^{(1.000288+\epsilon)/2}\tau(k)^{O(1)}/\sqrt{k}.
$$
For rational-squarefree columns only squarefree $k$ can contribute. Assign its primes to the unique coordinate and one of the two split primes. On the residual coordinates retain rational squarefreeness and omit the primes of $k$. Use the absolute $G/F$ convolution from the rational-squarefree comparison to reduce the residual moment by Minkowski to (1). Its coefficient mass with central normalization is $O_\epsilon(U^\epsilon)$ on the bounded polynomial scales, and all resulting scales decrease. Removing primes of $k$ from both local products is uniform. The same argument gives the unrestricted moment price (1) for rational-squarefree columns at any stated $L$.

Finally invert the threshold on the ordinary integer gcd. Its signed sector is $\sum_{k\ge U^{.2040}}(\mu_{\mathbb Z}*1_{[U^{.2040},\infty)})(k)\|P_k\|_2^2$; the coefficient is at most $\tau(k)$. Harmonic summation proves the claim. These projection bounds concern the actual factorized coefficients (and the specified rational-squarefree restriction). An arbitrary additional projection could destroy the short-inverse structure. On the uncolored rational-squarefree remainder from the [physical reduction](rational-short-inverse.md), one has $s=\log_U(L_{\rm col}/(Ng\,Nk))>.87794-.2040=.67394$ for the residual column width in the [dual decomposition](short-inverse-frequencies.md), where $L_{\rm col}$ denotes its norm scale. This gives two additional frequency cutoffs there. For every $a=\log_U Nw\le.24069$, write $(w)=w_0 v^6$ and $\nu=\log_U Nv\le a/6+o(1)$. If $\nu>0$ the residual widths contain a group $\nu\le p\le s/7+o(1)$. Indeed all widths below $\nu$ either reach $\nu$ with total at most $2\nu<s/7$, or leave at least seven labels of width at least $\nu$, since $s>6(.0987)+\nu$. For $\nu=0$ take the empty group. The [primitive-character bound (1)](short-product-moments.md#squarefree-norms-and-the-dual-character) then costs $M\le s+o(1)$, since $2a<5s/7$. This covers every dual element up to that norm at natural price. For sixth-power-free dual elements, $a\le.33783<s$ gives $\Phi(a,s)-s=(2a-s)_+/6<.000287$. It follows that those elements fit the $1.000287$ price, together with the earlier powerful range. These cutoffs use the smaller gcd threshold for the actual uncolored coefficients. The product of the rational primes occurring in just one of the two column norms now exceeds $U^{1.3478}$ for large $U$.
