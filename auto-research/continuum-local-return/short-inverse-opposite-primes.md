## The absolute dual norm shell

The same calculation applies after the [second Poisson formula](short-inverse-dual-dilation.md) on a smooth frequency shell $Nw\asymp A$. For the actual primitive conductor $d=(k\bar k)r_1r_2$ put $d_0=(Nk)^2R^2$, $R=L/(Ng\,Nk)$, and $q=Nd/d_0\asymp1$. The original Poisson scale is $U/Nj$ for $j\mid g$. Put
$$ V=Nj\,d_0/A=Nj\,L^2/((Ng)^2 A),\qquad v=\log_U V. $$
In particular $V$ is independent of $k$. For original radial weight $W$ and smooth compact absolute-frequency cutoff $F$, define $\widehat G_q(t)=F(qt)\widetilde W((U/V)t)$. On the compact positive annulus of the Fourier argument, $t^m\widetilde W^{(m)}(t)$ is bounded for every fixed $m$. Its inverse Fourier transform in the Eisenstein plane is uniformly radial Schwartz, as are all fixed derivatives in $\log q$. The primary additive character uses a fixed dual-lattice isometry/dilation which only changes the radial scale by a constant. For $U/V$ large the whole norm is rapidly decreasing. Primitive Fourier inversion gives $(U/V)$ times the restored element sum on $j$-multiples, with weight $G_q(Nu/V)$ and the literal primitive pair phase. The common factor $g$ has been removed from that phase; reinstalling its row character would spuriously annihilate the $j$-multiple terms. The residual ratio $q$ can be separated in the two original column norms with a common integrable Mellin majorant. Inverse and ordinary profiles can then be separated as above.
