# Sixth-power amplification of a genuine marked physical moment

Assume the preprint's $7/8$ theorem and the specified all-element moment. Let $u$ range over any subcollection of sixth-power-free elements of $\mathbf Z[\omega]$ with $Nu\asymp U$. The source's positive row sum at $H=U^M$, $M\ge1$, has bound $H U^\epsilon$ for the smooth ordinary pair (or the genuine inverse) and finitely many fixed prime factors. Assume this raw bound is valid also at all smaller ordinary or inverse scales, with the same prime factors, heights and fixed character. For a source restricted to nonexceptional inducing characters, impose its original restriction on $u$. Then the corresponding positive moment on the selected rows at $U$ is at most
$$
 U^{1+(5/6)(M-1)+\epsilon}.
$$
Any original polynomial height factors accompany the bound. Here the number of slots and the bounded exponent and test ranges are fixed before taking $U$ large, and the raw moment is required for the finitely many profile derivatives used below. If the row sum carries a positive smooth radial weight, choose a fixed admissible majorant bounded below on $N(ua^6)/H\ll1$. The source available in tracker #143 has plain range $[0,1/2]$ at unit width and admits the lowered scales for each plain label separately. Its inclusion predicate concerns only the primitive inducing character, besides $u\ne0$. The character at $u a^6$ coincides with that at $u$ outside the primes dividing $a$ and thus has the same inducing character. In particular a ramified prime outside the fixed bad primes persists. The physical prime weights obey $W(Np/P_i)=0$ outside $Np/P_i\in[1,2]$, even though the formal definition of the pool sums all smaller norms. Put $A=(H/U)^{1/6}$. For each fixed collection of $k$ raw prime slots, choose one dyadic log interval below $\log A$ disjoint from all their norm supports. In detail, take logarithms, where the forbidden intervals have total length at most $k\log C$ for a fixed support ratio $C$. An interval of length $k\log C+(k+2)\log 2$ below $\log A$ contains a complementary subinterval of length at least $\log 2$. Thus there is a full dyad $[A',2A']$ with $A'\asymp_{k,C}A$ in the complement (increase the fixed length for strict endpoints). For large $A$, prime ideal counting gives $\gg_{k,C} A/\log(2A)$ primary generators $a$ of prime ideals of norms in that dyad outside the fixed set. The implied constants can depend on the fixed number of slots and their support ratios, and are independent of their centers, subsets and coefficients. Write $\psi_u$ for the original zero-extended character with its permitted fixed omissions, and write $a$ also for the prime ideal generated by the chosen element. With central normalization, separating the exact $a$-valuation of each ordinary ideal gives
$$
 S_u(X;W)=\sum_{j\ge0}\frac{\psi_u(a)^j}{(Na)^{j/2}}

$$
If an original additional puncture divides $a$, include its zero in $\psi_u$. The coefficient mass is at most $(1-(Na)^{-1/2})^{-1}$. ## The physical ordinary face

With two equal ordinary log widths $n$, total canonical mark width $w$, and tracker constants $B=1-s,\lambda=2+10s,c=3/20-1000s/1143$, take
$$
 M=\max\{1,2n,w/c,(2n+\lambda w)/B\}.
$$
The mesh at the larger scale is preserved by homogeneity. At flat prime gain $q=\delta p$, the part of the positive energy exponent depending on $w$ is $(5/6)(M-1)-2qw$. In the stationary dyad, $n=x_0$ and $w_0=(B-2n)/\lambda$ is below the supply and cap, and $2n<1$. For $w\le w_0$ the exponent decreases with $w$. For $w\ge w_0$ it increases at least as fast as $(5/6)\lambda/B-2q\ge5/3-.4267>0$. Therefore the exact optimizing width and cost remain the stationary inherited values. The cap $w/c$, if reached later, only increases that slope. The same conclusion holds with the original preprint's coefficient $6\kappa$. At a native inverse prime face the two post-boundary slopes are respectively $5/3$ and $20/9$, again larger than the flat squared-prime gain. These slopes explain why this amplification leaves the flat stationary physical price unchanged. [Stationary dyad and measured gains](marked-window.md)

## Punctures in the eighteenth moment

In the full eighteenth moment write $u=u_0v^6$ and freeze $v$. The Euler product of the tested short inverse uses exactly the primes permitted by that presentation at $u_0v^6$. Its prime polynomial at $u_0$ therefore has the row-independent coefficient $1_{p\nmid v}$ in addition to its original fixed exclusions and the finite ray twist. The symbol itself retains its zero at every $p\mid u_0$. After disjoint coloring, each color profile has these same masks. On passing to $u_0a^6$, the extra zero at $p=a$ meets no prime with nonzero profile. This holds also if $a\mid v$ or $a\mid u_0$. The native inverse in the product comes from the zero detector on the underlying core family and keeps its own presentation. If an independent frozen puncture of polynomial norm is present in a genuine inverse, its Euler correction shifts the scale by $1/Nd$, with scalar at most $1/\sqrt{Nd}$, over $d$ supported on that puncture. Its absolute mass is bounded by $\prod_{p\mid B}(1-(Np)^{-1/2})^{-1}\ll_\epsilon (2NB)^\epsilon$. This uses only scale shortening and retains all prime-label masks.
